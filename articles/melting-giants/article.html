Melting Giants

Melting Giants is a composition for six instruments (flute, classical guitar, violin, cello, bassoon and bass clarinet), written for JA-HA-MU and premiered at Setkávání nové hudby plus: on 23 November 2026 at Atrium Žižkov in Prague and on 24 November 2026 in the Komorní sál JAMU in Brno. It lasts six to ten minutes.

The players perform from a text and their phones. The phone shows a field of water that freezes and melts as the piece goes. It is a mood to read, not a picture to illustrate. The audience can open the same page on their own phones, so the hall fills with small screens of ice and water, and their light becomes part of the score.

Two beings

The ensemble plays in three duos (flute and guitar, violin and cello, bassoon and bass clarinet), and each duo decides who becomes COSI and who becomes ČÍMSI. The only condition is that the two are not the same. Each being has its own text, and the players imagine themselves into it.

COSI are slow little beings, frozen in a grid, hidden in a shell, separated from the infinite outside. Fast beings from outside slowly break into their loneliness and charge them with energy. They step out and back, then further out, and learn to be open and warm, until they no longer go back inside. Only then do they see the giant they have been, and the giant they lost by becoming part of everything else.

ČÍMSI are fast, sharp-edged, warm beings: always on the move, full of energy, anxious. On their travels they find the small hidden creatures and want to be their friends. Every touch costs them a little warmth and speed, which they are happy to share. They grow calm, their limbs melt away, and their magma sets into a statue, frozen in time, only to be awakened again.

In Czech both words mean “something”. Cosi is the plain form, and čímsi is the same word in the instrumental case: “by means of something”. One is a thing, the other is what acts on it.

Pitch worlds

Each part of the text belongs to one of three worlds. COSI and ČÍMSI travel through them in opposite directions and meet in the middle:

  • COSI: Ice → Melting → Vapour
  • ČÍMSI: Vapour → Melting → Ice

Ice uses the whole-tone pitches C, D, E, F♯, G♯ and A♯. Each player chooses two or three of them in fixed octaves and plays them as pure tones without vibrato, mostly as single sounds followed by long stillness. Melting is where the two beings meet. Open D is a constant that anyone can return to, an Ice pitch may slide a semitone up and fall back, and close unisons are allowed to beat. Vapour allows anything (all pitches, microtones, any register), as long as no one holds a pitch together with anyone else.

The duos play only the first two parts and stop where they meet in Melting. Only the final tutti goes all the way through, so every being ends in the other's starting world. Between sections the silences are frozen, not rests: the players stay in position until the next cue.

The water

Each screen runs a small cellular automaton with three states: empty (black), liquid (blue), and ice (pale white). Liquid is born and dies by neighbourhood rules, much like Conway’s Game of Life. Liquid touching ice may freeze, and ice surrounded by enough liquid may melt.

A single phase value from 0 (cold) to 1 (warm) blends every rule between a frozen preset and a liquid one. When it’s cold, crystals spread slowly from any ice they find. When it’s warm, the field turns into fast, restless liquid and the ice dissolves.

The page asks one question: cosi or čímsi. The two groups move in opposite directions, like the beings in the score: when one warms and melts, the other cools and freezes, and back again. One control drives both, so the hall always holds a balance between ice and water.

Every phone starts with an irregular ice seed in the middle of the screen, a frozen blob that is shaped a little differently each time. What grows from it depends on the phase, on chance, and on whoever holds the phone: touching the screen pours liquid into your own field. No two phones show the same image.

Playing the room

During the performance the composer plays the phones live from a control page while the sextet plays. A slider sets the phase, and a glide time sets how long the change takes, from an instant snap to a ten-minute thaw. Commands can seed new ice, flash-freeze or melt everything, or wipe the field clean, for one group or for both.

All the phones follow the same clock, so a long glide stays in step across the hall, and someone who joins late lands in the middle of it instead of at the beginning.

Water / Words / Memory

The automaton comes from Water / Words / Memory, an ongoing series of sketches on cellular automata and generative poetry. They start with a binary automaton and move through liquid and ice states, phase blending, and interacting temperature fields toward particles that carry words. Melting Giants is the first time the series leaves the browser and enters a concert hall.
