Melting Giants

Melting Giants is a composition for six instruments (flute, classical guitar, violin, cello, bassoon and bass clarinet), written for JA-HA-MU and premiered on 23 November 2026 at Atrium Žižkov in Prague and on Setkávání nové hudby plus on 24 November 2026 in the Komorní sál JAMU in Brno.

The players perform from a text and their phones. The phone shows a field of water that freezes and melts as the piece goes. It is a mood to read, not a picture to illustrate. The audience can open the same page on their own phones, so the hall fills with small screens of ice and water, and their light becomes part of the score.

Two beings

The ensemble plays in three duos (flute and guitar, violin and cello, bassoon and bass clarinet), and each duo decides who becomes COSI and who becomes ČÍMSI. The only condition is that the two are not the same. Each being has its own text, and the players imagine themselves into it.

COSI

A] Slow little beings frozen in a grid alone hidden in a shell separated from the infinite outside Inside slowly being touched by the outside As a slow being you have DEEP CALM ---- depression coldness ..... straight-forwardness B] Outside beings penetrate your loneliness You start to see the wastness of universe outside is FAST so fast it charges you with energy you flow faster yourself curious about the outside you are no longer same changed as you are you explore step out; step back; two steps out three back; five; seven you learn to be open, warm, friendly you learn you don't want to be still no more until suddenly you don't go back inside anymore C] You were a slow little being or so you thought but now as you are fast an everywhere you see the GIANT you have been the GIANT you lost as you are now part of everything else You have made the inside-out transformation You are part of the environment now

ČÍMSI

A'] Fast sharp edges everywhere; always on the move full of energy; anxious warm beings On your travels you come by lonely small hidden creatures You are curious to be their friend You move your attention towards COSI B'] You interact with COSI hit it and lose a little bit of your warmness; speed You are happy to share the heat You gain calmness and wisdom by becoming friends C'] You no longer strive to be everywhere and see all you feel calm and chill after such a long time you breathe in a stedy rythm your arms and legs melted away become stone your magma becomes a statue forever fixed in time as you die; freeze only to be awaken again

In Czech both words mean “something”. Cosi is the plain form, and čímsi is the same word in the instrumental case: “by means of something”. One is a thing, the other is what acts on it.

Pitch worlds

Each part of the text belongs to one of three worlds. COSI and ČÍMSI travel through them in opposite directions and meet in the middle:

  • COSI: Ice → Melting → Vapour
  • ČÍMSI: Vapour → Melting → Ice

Ice uses the whole-tone pitches C, D, E, F♯, G♯ and A♯. Each player chooses two or three of them in fixed octaves and plays them as pure tones without vibrato, mostly as single sounds followed by long stillness. Melting is where the two beings meet. Open D is a constant that anyone can return to, an Ice pitch may slide a semitone up and fall back, and close unisons are allowed to beat. Vapour allows anything (all pitches, microtones, any register), as long as no one holds a pitch together with anyone else.

The duos play only the first two parts and stop where they meet in Melting. Only the final tutti goes all the way through, so every being ends in the other's starting world. Between sections the silences are frozen, not rests: the players stay in position until the next cue.

The water

Each screen runs a small cellular automaton with three states: empty (black), liquid (blue), and ice (pale white). Liquid is born and dies by neighbourhood rules, much like Conway’s Game of Life. Liquid touching ice may freeze, and ice surrounded by enough liquid may melt.

A single phase value from 0 (cold) to 1 (warm) blends every rule between a frozen preset and a liquid one. When it’s cold, crystals spread slowly from any ice they find. When it’s warm, the field turns into fast, restless liquid and the ice dissolves.

The page asks one question: cosi or čímsi. The two groups move in opposite directions, like the beings in the score: when one warms and melts, the other cools and freezes, and back again. One control drives both, so the hall always holds a balance between ice and water.

Every phone starts with an irregular ice seed in the middle of the screen, a frozen blob that is shaped a little differently each time. What grows from it depends on the phase, on chance, and on whoever holds the phone: touching the screen pours liquid into your own field. No two phones show the same image.

Playing the room

During the performance the composer plays the phones live from a control page while the sextet plays. A slider sets the phase, and a glide time sets how long the change takes, from an instant snap to a ten-minute thaw. Commands can seed new ice, flash-freeze or melt everything, or wipe the field clean, for one group or for both.

All the phones follow the same clock, so a long glide stays in step across the hall, and someone who joins late lands in the middle of it instead of at the beginning.

Two concerts

During both concerts the control page saved a snapshot every ten seconds: how many phones were on the page in each group, how many of them were being touched, and where on the screen. The two halls played the piece very differently.

In Atrium Žižkov about ten phones were on the page during the piece, never more than eleven at once, and they stayed until the end. Hardly anyone in the cosi group touched the screen: their four or five phones were touched for only about two minutes in the middle, and the čímsi phones did nine tenths of all the touching. The room mostly watched the water.

In the Komorní sál JAMU more than twice as many phones joined: 23 at the peak, early in the piece, seventeen of them cosi. In every snapshot someone was touching a screen, at the busiest moment thirteen phones at once, and the hall touched about five times as much as in Prague, two thirds of it in the cosi group. Then the crowd thinned out, and by the end twelve phones were left.

So the touch mattered more in Brno. With the shared glow and the crowd nudge switched on, the audience's touch shows on every screen in the hall and pushes its own group's phase. In Prague the crowd moved the phase by at most 0.04 on top of the composer's slider. In Brno it moved it by 0.10, a tenth of the whole way from ice to water.

Studies

The automaton comes from the Melting Giants studies, an ongoing series of sketches on cellular automata and generative poetry. They start with a binary automaton and move through liquid and ice states, phase blending, and interacting temperature fields toward particles that carry words. The piece is the first time the series leaves the browser and enters a concert hall.
